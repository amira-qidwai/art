Ceramics Growth

Ceramics is a challenging but rewarding medium. This timeline explores my experience with ceramics beginning in 2018.

2018
I became interested in ceramics in 2018 and started taking classes at my high school and at a community ceramics studio. This was the first time I had the chance to use equipment like a pottery wheel. The image above is some of the first items I ever got off the wheel. I recycled most of the first pieces I threw on the wheel to continue using the clay for practice, but the few pieces I did successfully finish are shown below.
2022
Some of the first wheel-thrown pieces I finished are shown above. The bowl on the left has extremely thick walls, a thin base, and was thrown off center but I love it, and I continue to use it as a water bowl when I’m painting. On the right are a couple of mugs I threw and used to practice pulling handles. They are tiny, more like espresso cups, because I didn’t really understand clay shrinkage when I made them. I continued taking classes at my high school until 2022, when I graduated, and no longer had access to a ceramics studio.
2024
I took a break from ceramics for a couple of years as I started college. In 2024 I re-enrolled in community ceramic art classes at the Robert E. Lee Recreation Center in Iowa City. I took an eight-week course there and made quite a few items for myself and my family. My collection from this class is shown in the image above. Below are a couple of more challenging forms that I learned to throw on the wheel including a dinner plate and a jug. The jug, again, was a lesson in pulling handles and clay shrinkage. The handle was too thick for the form and after two rounds of firing it was only seven inches tall, but it serves as a nice vase now. The plate was well thrown and carved but I used a questionable glaze on it that bubbled and pooled, so I now affectionately call it “the green blob plate”. It is not food safe, unless you want to eat tiny glass shards.
2025
I started the year by taking two more classes at the Rec Center and improved my throwing and hand building skills quite a bit. Below are pictures of some of the pieces I made during these classes, including two of my favorite hand-built pieces that I use every day. On the right is a bedside shelf that sits on my nightstand and below a ceramic organizer that holds my cosmetics. My throwing skills also excelled as I focused on throwing more complicated forms as evidenced by the box of vases shown below. Throwing the wide bellies and long necks of these vases was a challenge and a great learning experience. Beneath these images are a couple of videos exploring the process of making some of the ceramic objects available on my website. Since these classes, I have focused on other mediums, but I am excited to progress more in my ceramics skills this fall as I take my first university level course in wheel throwing.
