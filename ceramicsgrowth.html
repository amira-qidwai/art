Ceramics Growth

Ceramics is a challenging but rewarding medium. This timeline explores my experience with ceramics beginning in 2018.

2018
I first became interested in ceramics in 2018, when I began taking classes at my high school and at a community studio. It was my first time using equipment like a pottery wheel, and the pieces above are some of the earliest items I managed to throw successfully. Most of my first attempts were recycled, but a few finished pieces survived. They’re shown above.
2022
These early wheel-thrown pieces shown above have plenty of flaws, but they still make themselves useful. The bowl on the left has thick walls, an uneven base, and was thrown off-center—but I love it, and I still use it as a water bowl when I’m painting. On the right are a couple of tiny mugs I made while practicing pulling handles. They’re basically espresso cups because I didn’t yet understand how much clay shrinks during firing. I continued taking classes at my high school until graduating in 2022, when I no longer had access to a ceramics studio for free.
2024
After starting college, I took a break from ceramics for a couple of years. In 2024, I re-enrolled in community ceramic classes and completed an eight-week course. The collection from that class is shown above. Below are two of the more challenging forms I learned to throw: a dinner plate and a jug. The jug was another lesson in handle-making and clay shrinkage—the handle ended up too thick for the farm, and after two firings the jug stood only seven inches tall. It now serves as a small vase. The plate was well-thrown and carved, but I used a questionable glaze that bubbled and pooled. I now affectionately call it “the green blob plate.” It is not food-safe unless you enjoy eating tiny shards of glass.
Spring 2025
In Spring 2025, I took two more community ceramics classes and continued improving both my wheel-throwing and hand-building skills. The pieces shown below are from those courses, including two of my favorite hand-built items that I use every day: a bedside shelf and a ceramic organizer for my cosmetics. My throwing skills also grew as I practiced more complex form, like the vases shown below. Throwing wide bellies and long necks was a challenge, but a rewarding one. During this time, I also made a few videos documenting the process behind several ceramic pieces displayed on my website.

Fall 2025
In late 2025, I took a beginner wheel-throwing class at the University of Iowa. I improved significantly and experimented with a variety of techniques. My final project—a table setting inspired by connections between Indian, Pakistani, and Bengali cultures—is shown on the left. I also completed an honors project consisting of 24 spice jars, inspired by the Lenox Spice Village. More information about this project is available on its product page, which can be reached by clicking on the image below.
2026
In early 2026, I took another community ceramics class in Iowa City. This time, I focused on having a clear shape and decoration plan for each piece before throwing it. Some of my sketches and plans are shown below. I made a full dish set for my apartment, along with several pieces for my family and for listing here. Many works from this period feature techniques that have become central to my ceramic style, such as floral rims and mehndi/henna designs piped onto forms. Since May 2026, I’ve had to pause ceramics because I no longer have access to a studio space, but I’m excited to return to clay in the future.
