Frankenstein Feminine Sublime Study

Not For Sale

Medium: Cotton Fabric and Cotton Embroidery Thread

Dimensions: 8"Dia.

Description: Embroidery work exploring symbols of the sublime in Mary Shelley's novel "Frankenstein." Negative space silhouette of Mary Shelley dominates the middle of composition surrounded by examples of the sublime included in "Frankenstein" such as nature and the creation of life represented by arctic and countryside landscapes as well as arm anatomy.

Further Information: I created this piece for a final project in a literature course during college. I made the argument in my essay that Mary Shelley brought the sublimity of many concepts to life in her novel by including and excluding them. She included rich descriptions of the landscapes surrounding her characters but she excludes women and feminine energy from most of her novel. Shelley demonstrates the sublimity of women and femininity by largely excluding them, most notably when the Creature insists Victor make him a feminine companion and Victor never finishes the process. The female silhouette in the center of this piece, modeled off a portrait of Shelley, represents this exclusion surrounded by the sublime themes that were deliberately included.
